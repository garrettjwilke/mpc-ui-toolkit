# Swallows and martins

Swallows and martins are the family Hirundinidae. They are oscine passerines built for feeding in flight. The bill is short and the gape is wide. The wings are long and pointed. The legs are short, and the foot is still the passerine foot, used on a wire, a reed, or the rim of a nest. [Oscines](oscines.md) is the group. [Crows](corvidae.md) are the large walking relatives. This page is the aerial one.

## In the air

A swallow takes insects in flight. The wide mouth is the net. The bird drinks and even bathes on the wing, dipping to the water. Flight is fluid, with quick turns, not the stiff flaps of a swift. Swifts look similar and are not passerines. They have a different wing, a different foot, and a different order. A [swift](swift.md) has no page here, and it should not, because it is outside Passeriformes. The comparison is the one people get wrong in the field.

Martins are the same family. The name is English, not a separate rank. House martins, sand martins, and crag martins are swallows in the broad sense. River martins and the larger New World martins, including the purple martin, belong here too. None of those species has a page. The family does.

## Nests

Mud is the famous material. A barn swallow builds a cup of mud and straw on a beam or a ledge, open at the top. A house martin builds under an eave and closes the cup until a hole remains. Sand martins dig a tunnel in a bank. Tree swallows use holes. The [nests](nests.md) page is the order's general account. Swallows are the reason that page mentions mud.

Colonies are common where the nesting sites are clumped: a bank of sand martin holes, a bridge of house martin cups, a barn with several barn swallows. The birds still form pairs. A colony is many nests, not one shared nest.

## Migration

Many swallows are long-distance migrants. A barn swallow breeding in Europe can spend the other half of the year in southern Africa. The trip is a passerine doing what a passerine's wing allows when the wing is this long. Not every species migrates. Some tropical swallows stay. The family is not defined by the journey. It is defined by the flight-feeding body and the relationships that put these birds together.

Roosts before migration can hold thousands of birds in a reedbed. The noise is a passerine chorus of a different kind from a dawn song. [Song](song.md) is mostly about territorial phrases. Swallows do sing, a twittering that carries less far than a thrush. The roost sound is calls, stacked until it is the loudest thing in the marsh.

## The foot you forget

Because the bird is almost always in the air or on a wire, people forget it perches with three toes forward and one back. It can sleep on that wire for the same tendon reason as a [sparrow](sparrows.md). The leg is just shorter, so the bird looks stuck to the wire rather than standing on it. [Description](description.md) is the foot. This family is the test that the foot survives in a bird that almost never walks.
