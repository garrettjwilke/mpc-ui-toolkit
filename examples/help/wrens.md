# New Zealand wrens

The New Zealand wrens, family Acanthisittidae, are passerines, and they are the sister group to every other living passerine. They are not oscines. They are not suboscines. [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) draws that three-way split. [Origin](origin.md) is why a handful of New Zealand birds sit on their own branch.

## Living birds

Two species are still easy to find if you are in the right forest. The rifleman is a tiny green bird that climbs trunks, male and female slightly different, the bill thin and slightly upturned. It nests in a hole and lays a large clutch for its size. The rock wren lives in alpine boulder fields, bobs, and hides in crevices. Both are weak fliers compared with a warbler. Both have the passerine foot. Neither sings like a thrush, because the elaborate learned song is an oscine trait. [Song](song.md) is that other trait. These birds are the comparison that sits outside it.

"Wren" here is an English habit of naming small brown or green birds after the European wren. The European wren is a troglodytid, an oscine, and it has no page in this sample. The shared name is not a relationship. [Oscines](oscines.md) is where that other wren would belong.

## What was lost

Other acanthisittids lived into the period after people reached New Zealand, and then they did not. The bush wren and the Stephens Island wren are the often-cited extinctions. The Stephens Island bird is part of a famous story about a lighthouse cat. The details of that story get repeated past the evidence, so this page will not pretend to a precise tally of cats. The solid point is smaller and sadder: several members of this oldest passerine branch disappeared in historical time, and the branch was already species-poor.

Subfossil bones show the family was a little more varied before that, including forms that were more terrestrial. Islands that lacked mammal predators produced passerines that gave up some flight. When predators arrived, those birds had no time to change. The rifleman and the rock wren are the ones that remain.

## Why they matter to the order

If these birds had all vanished before anyone compared their DNA and their anatomy with the rest of the order, the passerines would look like two groups. The wrens are the evidence of a third, older split. They are not ancestors of sparrows. A sparrow did not descend from a rifleman. Both descended from an older passerine, and the rifleman's line kept its own course on an island.

That is the same shape as the southern origin told on the [origin](origin.md) page. The [suboscines](suboscines.md) are the New World branch of the remaining tree. The oscines are the other branch. The wrens are what you have to account for before you tell either of those stories.
