# Nests and eggs

Most passerines raise their young in a nest, and most of those nests are cups. The chick hatches with its eyes shut and almost no down, and it stays until it can grip a twig and call for food. That plan is common in the order and not universal. [Brood parasites](brood.md) skip the nest of their own. Swallows build with mud. Some wrens build a dome. This page is the ordinary case. [Description](description.md) is the adult that comes back to feed.

## The cup

A cup nest is grass, rootlets, moss, or strips of bark, worked into a circle and lined with hair, feathers, or fine fibre. The bird uses its body as the mould, turning and pressing. Spider silk is a common binder for the smallest species, because a nest of loose grass would fall apart at that scale. The nest sits in a fork, on a ledge, in a hole, or on the ground under a tussock.

Hole nesters use a cavity they did not always excavate. Tits, nuthatches, and starlings take old woodpecker holes or a box. Nuthatches may plaster the entrance with mud until it fits them and not a larger rival. Starlings have no page here. The habit is still a passerine habit: the eggs are in a cup or a hollow, and the young are fed.

[Swallows](swallows.md) are the mud builders. A barn swallow's cup is pellets of mud and straw stuck to a beam. A house martin closes the cup until a small hole is left. The foot that grips a twig also grips the rim of that nest. The bird is no less a passerine for nesting on a building.

## Eggs

Passerine eggs are usually pale, spotted, and small. A clutch might be three eggs in the tropics or five to seven in a temperate spring. The spots are pigment laid on as the egg moves in the shell gland. Hole nesters often lay paler eggs. Open-cup nesters often lay eggs that match the lining. The pattern is not an identification you can trust across the order, but it is a pattern.

Incubation is often by the female, sometimes by both parents. It lasts roughly ten days to two weeks in small species, longer in the large corvids. A raven's egg is a different object from a warbler's, and the time on the nest matches that. [Crows](corvidae.md) are slow, for this order. A small warbler is fast.

Hatching is staggered or synchronized depending on when incubation started. If the female begins with the first egg, the chicks differ in age and the smallest may starve when food is short. If she waits until the clutch is complete, they hatch together. Both strategies occur in passerines. Neither is the brood-parasite strategy. That one is on the [brood parasite](brood.md) page: the egg goes into someone else's cup.

## The young

Chicks beg with bright mouth linings. A yellow or orange gape in a dark nest is a target for the parent. The parent arrives with an insect, or a bolus of insects, and pushes it into the brightest mouth. Faecal sacs are carried away or eaten, which keeps the nest harder to smell and to see.

A small songbird fledges in about two weeks. The fledgling can flutter and grip, and it cannot feed itself. Parents follow it into cover. A person who finds a grey, short-tailed bird on the ground in June has often found a fledgling, not a nestling that needs to be put back. The [sparrow](sparrows.md) fledgling is a common case: duller than the male, shaking its wings, still being fed.

Second broods are common when the season is long. Tropical pairs may nest again and again. Arctic and high-mountain pairs may have one chance. The nest is abandoned after fledging. Passerines do not return to a cup the way some large birds return to a stick platform for decades, though a good ledge or hole may be reused and rebuilt.

## What is not a passerine nest

A woodpecker hole is excavated by a bird that is not a passerine, then used by one. A cuckoo egg in a warbler nest belongs to a bird outside the order, laid into a passerine cup. The host is the passerine. The parasite in that famous case is not. Some parasites inside the order are covered under [brood parasites](brood.md). Weavers, ovenbirds, and oropendolas build famous nests and have no pages here. Their names can sit in the sentence. They cannot be selected.
