# Crows, ravens, and jays

Corvidae is a family of oscine passerines: crows, ravens, rooks, jackdaws, jays, magpies, treepies, and nutcrackers. They are among the largest passerines, and they are songbirds in the taxonomic sense. The voice is often harsh. The syrinx is still the oscine one. [Oscines](oscines.md) is the group. [Song](song.md) is why a caw still counts.

## The birds

A carrion crow is all black, walks as much as it hops, and will work a mussel, a carcass, or a bin. A rook is the social one of the black crows, nesting in a rookery, with a bare pale face as an adult. A jackdaw is smaller, grey on the neck, and fond of chimneys. A raven is larger, with a heavier bill and a wedge-shaped tail, and it soars more than the crows do. These are species-level differences inside one family. None of the species has its own page. The family does.

Jays are the colourful or the barred members. The Eurasian jay buries acorns and remembers enough of them to matter to the oak. The blue jay of North America is a different bird with a similar habit of noise and of food caches. Magpies have long tails and, in the familiar black-and-white species, a gloss that looks green or blue when it turns. Nutcrackers specialize on conifer seeds and cache them in the thousands. A [nutcracker](nucifraga.md) has no page. It is a corvid all the same.

## Brains, caches, and company

Corvids cache food, steal caches, and change their behaviour when another bird can see them. That set of habits has made them a favourite of people who study animal minds. The useful caution is that "intelligent" is not a family character you can tick in a key. It is a description of experiments on particular species, mostly ravens, crows, and jays. A bird that solves a puzzle in a lab is still a passerine with the standard foot.

They are social in different degrees. Jackdaws and rooks are rarely alone. Ravens gather at food and at roosts and still nest as pairs. Jays are often seen as pairs. Young birds travel in loose groups. The family is not one social system. It is several, built on a body that is large for a passerine and a bill that can handle more than insects.

## Nests and range

Corvids build stick cups, usually high. The clutch is smaller than a warbler's, the incubation longer, and the young stay dependent longer. [Nests and eggs](nests.md) is the general passerine pattern. Corvids stretch it toward fewer, slower young. They live on every continent except Antarctica, and they are missing from New Zealand as a native family. People have introduced them in places. The native New Zealand passerines of interest in this sample are the [wrens](wrens.md), which are not corvids.

Diet is broad. Insects, grain, eggs, fruit, carrion, and other birds' chicks all appear. A raven at a carcass and a jay in an oak are using the same generalist bill at different scales. They are not raptors. They have no talons. The foot is the perching foot, strong enough to hold food down.

[Sparrows](sparrows.md) are the namesake of the order, much closer to a crow in being a walking, seed-capable bird than a swallow is, and much smaller.
