# Oscines

Oscines are the songbirds in the strict sense: passerines with a complex syrinx and a song that is learned. They are the group called Passeri. Most passerine species are oscines. [Song](song.md) describes the learning. [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) places the group beside the suboscines and the New Zealand wrens. This page is about how the oscines are divided, and which divisions this sample can open.

## Corvides

The crow-like cluster includes the crows, jays, magpies, and nutcrackers, and also a wide set of relatives that do not look like crows. Shrikes, orioles of the Old World, drongos, fantails, monarchs, birds of paradise, and vireos sit in this neighbourhood. Many of the Australian songbirds do too. The cluster is one branch of the oscines, not a single family.

[Crows, ravens, and jays](corvidae.md) is the family this sample opens. A [shrike](laniidae.md), a [bird of paradise](paradisaeidae.md), and a [vireo](vireonidae.md) are named so the branch is not only corvids. They have no pages. The wheel skips them. The corvid page is the one that answers.

## Passerida

The other great oscine cluster holds the birds a northern field guide is mostly made of. Larks and swallows are an early piece of it. Wagtails and pipits sit nearby. Then come the tits, the penduline tits, the long-tailed tits, several warbler families, the creepers, the nuthatches, the wrens of the Americas and the north, the dippers, the thrushes, the Old World flycatchers, the starlings, and the sparrows. Finches are here. So are the American warblers, tanagers, cardinals, blackbirds, and buntings, which are oscines even though they live alongside suboscines.

[Swallows](swallows.md) and [sparrows](sparrows.md) are the two Passerida pages in this sample. A [lark](alaudidae.md), a [thrush](turdidae.md), a [finch](fringillidae.md), and a [warbler](phylloscopidae.md) are ordinary text. You can read the names. You cannot open them.

## Why the songs differ inside one group

All of these birds are oscines, and they do not sound alike. A swallow's song is a quiet twitter. A wren's is a shout. A crow's is a caw that still comes from a learned, muscled syrinx. The classification is not a ranking of musical value. It is a statement about muscles and about who inherited them. A beautiful suboscine song, and there are some, does not move that bird into this group. [Suboscines](suboscines.md) is the comparison.

Within oscines, some species copy for life and some close the window. Some females sing as much as males. Tropical pairs often both sing. Temperate migrants often leave the long song to the male on the territory. Those are habits inside the group, not a reason to split it.
